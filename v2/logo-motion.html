Yes — the rotation is added in code, not in the file

Your Canva export contains no animation at all: no animate tags, no keyframes, no script. That is normal — Canva only exports movement as MP4 or GIF, never as an animated SVG. It doesn't matter. I take the still vector shapes and add the motion on the site, which is better anyway: it stays razor-sharp, weighs nothing, and I can switch it off for people who need it off. Two practical warnings before you commit to constant movement.
A logo that never stops moving is the one element on every page that always draws the eye, and on a news site you want that eye on the headline. My advice is to let it animate once on page load and then rest — or keep it slow, like the sweeping clock above.

Second, some people get motion sickness or migraines from looped animation, and their device carries a "reduce motion" setting to say so. Every option on this page already respects it: turn that setting on and the movement stops by itself. That is a few lines of CSS, and it is the difference between a professional build and an amateur one.
